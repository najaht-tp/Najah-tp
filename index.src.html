ACT (Atria Convergence Technologies) Fibernet

ACT Fibernet: Award‑Winning Mobile Experience

ACT Fibernet is a leading broadband service provider that offers high-speed internet connectivity across India.
Client
ACT Fibernet
Location
India
Services
User Research, Mobile UI/UX Design + Development
Type
App Design

Brief

ACT Fibernet is a leading broadband service provider known for delivering high-speed internet across various regions across India. To further enhance its market position and provide a seamless digital experience, ACT Fibernet collaborated with us to design and develop a mobile application. Some of the key features of the design were

  • Implementing cross-selling techniques and promoting new products.
  • Enhancing feature discoverability and user education on WiFi and routers.
  • Gaining insights into customer expectations for tailored experiences.
  • Developing a modular and flexible app architecture.
  • Aligning the ACT brand with users’ cultural and language preferences.
  • Optimizing frequently used transactions and promoting feature exploration.

Discover

To understand user needs and improve the ACT app, we facilitated workshops and user interviews with stakeholders and end-users. Our research aimed to identify current usage, understanding, and expectations. Key findings included:

  • Onboarding

    Users faced confusion and a lack of guidance, leading to a poor initial experience.

  • Flexibility

    Limited options reduced users’ ability to quickly resolve issues.

  • Payments

    Inconsistent payment flow and lack of trust and transparency led users to seek alternative methods.

  • CRP Claims

    Ambiguity and data privacy concerns made users rely on word of mouth instead of app features.

  • CRP Value-Added Services (VAS)

    Users need more information to feel confident in their decisions.

Define

Our primary focus was to revamp the ACT customer app to align with the brand’s vision and marketing positioning. We defined multiple problem statements to address through design, by leveraging user research insights, user personas, journey maps, competitor analysis, information architecture, and task flows.

Taking into consideration all these points, our aim was to enhance user engagement by including cross-selling, understanding customer expectations, expanding the app while maintaining performance, meeting design preferences, ensuring flexibility for future features, and simplifying common transactions for user convenience.

With a clear approach, we mapped out the information architecture to illustrate flow across platforms and connectivity. Based on the user journeys and prerequisites, we introduced exceptional and distinctive features within the app, aiming to deliver an enhanced user experience.

User personas

Wireframes

Once we finalized the information architecture through multiple iterations, we proceeded with creating wireframes. We developed high-fidelity wireframes and later progressed to the visual design stage. It was crucial to address the pain points and opportunities of each persona while adhering to the aforementioned considerations.

We prioritized a smooth and straightforward onboarding process, followed by designing an intuitive, dynamic, and visually appealing homepage. Ensuring the app was feature-rich yet simple to use for all personas was a key focus. Additionally, we redesigned the entire experience for managing plans and value-added services (VAS) by employing familiar mental models to ensure usability for every persona.

Brand guidelines

Half-filled icons are simple yet depthful, they convey information effectively with a clean aesthetic, enhancing user engagement.

Colours

Both primary and secondary colors have been used in the application to establish a strong brand connection. These colors are derived from the brand guidelines and logo, ensuring consistency across the design.

Sid’s Farm

Sid’s Farm is a premium dairy brand that delivers fresh, high-quality milk and dairy products to the doorstep. This case study follows the app from discovery and UX research through information architecture and wireframes to the final UI design.

Connect Me

UX & UI Design

Overview

Sid’s Farm tests its products rigorously to meet strict quality standards and is built on purity and sustainability. The brief was to redesign the app so that it feels intuitive, engaging and personal: simple navigation, customisable subscriptions, easy order management and a short checkout, with the farm’s story and values coming through everywhere.

Discovery

I started with a discovery report, the first document of the design process. It gathered secondary research, brought together what the team knew about the product, and collected enough evidence to decide what to do next. It covered the brand, a client workshop, and user personas.

Client requirements

Short-term goals

  • Improve the app’s experience and usability, so users reach their goal with less effort
  • Improve storytelling, so the brand’s narrative and values come across
  • Increase engagement and conversions by letting users shape their own experience

Long-term goals

  • Scale the app by introducing new dairy products for different customer segments, and promoting them next to the best sellers
  • Expand the business into larger urban areas, to grow customers and sales

Workshop insights

A workshop with the client turned those goals into focus areas for marketing, business and customer support.

Marketing

  • FOMO factor: create a “fear of missing out” to lift engagement and app use
  • Referral programme to turn users into advocates
  • Farm visit: show the farm to build transparency and trust
  • Gamification to keep users coming back and feeling valued
  • Hyper-customisation for cross-selling related products

Business

  • Focused storytelling: chemical-free products, nothing hidden
  • Product discoverability: a personalised shopping experience for premium customers
  • Customer loyalty: be open and engaging, so users become repeat customers

Customer support

  • Help and support that builds trust and encourages word of mouth
  • Delivery tracking and delivery customisation, so problems are avoided early
  • Notifications for new products, delivery updates and new features
  • Wallet for faster, simpler payments

Who we designed for

The research defined five user groups and fifteen detailed personas with their goals, challenges, behaviours and tech comfort.

  • The Elders55+, tradition-focused retirees who want simplicity and value for money
  • The Family Builders30 to 55, practical parents focused on family health and saving time
  • The Professionals24 to 30, health-conscious and busy, they value convenience
  • The Deal Hunters18 to 24, fitness-focused young adults always looking for the best deal
  • The Explorers12 to 18, trend-following teens looking for fun and new things
  • “I just want something simple, affordable, and easy to use without any trouble.” (Ramesh, 62, retired teacher)
  • “I need products I can trust and services that make life easier, not more complicated.” (Priya, working mother)
  • “I don’t mind paying more as long as it’s quick, easy, and high-quality.” (Sneha, IT professional)

Decisions from the IA

  • Let people look before they commit. “Just Browse” skips sign-in; the app asks users to log in or sign up only when they add something to the basket.
  • Sign in with fewer hurdles. Mobile number and OTP, with the OTP sent by WhatsApp when the number has an account, or by SMS when it does not.
  • One basket for one-time and subscription orders. Daily, alternate-day, weekly or custom delivery, with a calendar for dates and holiday pauses.
  • A wallet for subscriptions. The app explains why subscriptions are paid from the wallet, and offers auto-recharge, promo codes and refund tracking.
  • Delivery that can be customised. Instructions with notes and a photo, live tracking, and clear handling of failed or partial deliveries.

Wireframes

Each main journey was wireframed state by state before any visual design. These are the screens that carry the app: home, browsing, subscribing and delivery.

Design approach

Simple & clear

A minimal, functional interface that feels effortless: less text, a conversational tone, and real-life photography with hand-drawn illustrations for warmth.

Visual warmth

Trust and comfort through soft pastel backgrounds and hand-drawn illustrations that wrap around the products in banners and promotions.

Playful

Joy and curiosity through dynamic layouts, blobs and interactive elements that make every tap and scroll feel delightful.

UI designs

Summary

The problem

A premium dairy brand wanted an app that is easy to use, tells its farm story, and turns first-time visitors into regular subscribers.

The people

Five user groups, from teens to seniors, who all value trust, freshness and convenience, and who differ in how comfortable they are with technology.

The approach

A discovery report and client workshop, then personas, an information architecture, wireframes for every main flow, and a design system before the final UI.

The solution

A warm, simple app with guest browsing, one basket for one-time and subscription orders, a wallet, customisable delivery, rewards, and support in one place.

Result

The project delivered a complete app design for Sid’s Farm: ten feature areas, from onboarding, categories and ordering to wallet, delivery, account, community, loyalty and support, all built on one design system. Each client goal is answered by a specific part of the design.

  • Better experience and usabilityGuest browsing, a short sign-in, one basket for every order type, and clear steps from product to payment.
  • Stronger storytellingWelcome screens built on safety and trust, brand stories on the home screen, quality reports and farm-visit certificates.
  • More engagement and conversionsSid’s Tokens, tiers and referrals, with the subscription discount shown right where users choose how to buy.
  • A way to scaleReusable horizontal and vertical product cards, so new dairy products and festive ranges fit in without a redesign.
  • Trust after the orderDelivery tracking with photo instructions, wallet refund tracking, WhatsApp help and a ticket status screen.

What I took from this project

  • Five very different user groups still wanted the same thing: a short, simple path. Features were added behind that path, never in front of it.
  • Trust is a design material. Proof (quality reports, the farm, delivery photos) did more than copy could.
  • A shared design system made it easy to add new flows such as loyalty and support without the app feeling stitched together.

Sony LIV · Product design · OTT streaming

Reimagining content discovery. Simplifying the experience.

Transforming Sony LIV’s complex design ecosystem and improving content discovery through intuitive, accessible and engaging cross-platform experiences.

  • Android
  • iOS
  • Tablet
  • iPad
  • Web
  • TV
Connect Me
Client
Sony LIV
Scope
Design system, New & Hot, Search
My role
Product Designer, UI/UX
Platforms
Android, iOS, Tablet, iPad, Web, TV

The brief

Sony LIV brought three product design challenges to our team. The existing experience had usability and discoverability issues in important parts of the platform. The goal was to simplify, make content easier to find, and keep one consistent experience on every device.

01

A complex design system

Hard for designers and developers to navigate, understand and use consistently.

02

A section nobody noticed

New & Hot was not getting the attention it deserved, so new and trending content was easy to miss.

03

A search that expected a query

Users dropped off before searching, and the page did little for people with no title in mind.

Complexity→Simplicity

Low discoverability→Engaging discovery

Empty search→Content exploration

Understanding the users

People open Sony LIV with very different intentions, and search shows it most clearly. These two behaviours are design considerations that shaped the work. They are not findings from validated research.

Design hypothesis

Intentional searchers

They already know what they want: a movie, a show, an actor, a sport or a genre. They need a fast path with as little typing and tapping as possible.

Design hypothesis

Exploratory searchers

They open search to see what is on. They need inspiration, trending content and recommendations. Without them, there is no reason to stay.

Don’t make users think about what to search. Give them a reason to explore.

Design strategy

Every decision in the project was checked against the same principles.

  • SimplicityFewer parts, clearer names, less to learn.
  • DiscoverabilitySurface what is new, trending and upcoming.
  • AccessibilityReadable text and clear focus on every device.
  • Visual hierarchyOne clear thing to look at first.
  • ConsistencyThe same language from phone to TV.
  • ScalabilityComponents that grow without breaking.
  • Low frictionFewer steps between intent and content.
  • Cross-platformPlatform-aware, not stretched.

Solution 01: Design system simplification

From a complicated design ecosystem to a simplified, accessible and scalable design foundation.

The system was hard to navigate, and designers and developers struggled to find, understand and apply its components the same way. Sony LIV wanted one foundation that both groups could use easily.

What we did

  • Reviewed the existing component architecture and looked for complexity and inconsistency.
  • Reorganised the system into Foundations (colours, grids and spacing, typography, icons, illustrations, avatars), Components and Patterns.
  • Documented each component in the same way: its components, anatomy and spacing, guidelines and references.
  • Linked design to code language with variables for colour, type, spacing and radius, so designers and developers use the same names.
  • Rolled the work out in phases, and logged every change in a changelog.

The structure

Instead of one long list, the system is split into three clear layers. Each part has a clear place, so people know where to look.

Layer 1

Foundations

  • Colours
  • Grids and spacing
  • Typography
  • Icons
  • Illustrations
  • Avatars
Layer 2

Components

  • Buttons
  • Bottom navigation
  • Tabs and chips
  • Input and search fields
  • Selection controls
  • Badges, toast, tooltip
  • Loader states
Layer 3

Patterns

  • Top app bar
  • Thumbnails and trays
  • Spotlight
  • Listing
  • Left navigation
  • Modals and accordions
  • Error and success states

The same documentation for every component

Each component page has four parts: Components (every state), Anatomy and spacing, Guidelines and Research and references. States are shown for mobile, tablet and web, and TV side by side, so nobody has to guess how a component behaves on another device.

Built so designers and developers share one language

  • Colour is organised as primitives with semantic roles on top, and the black, white and grey neutrals were merged into one tone.
  • Typography has its own variable modes for mobile, iPad and web, and TV.
  • Spacing and radius are variables linked to components, so a change flows through everywhere.
  • The roll-out was planned in phases, and every change is recorded in a changelog.

Solution 02: New & Hot redesign

Turning an overlooked content destination into an engaging discovery experience.

New & Hot was not getting the attention it deserved. It needed stronger presence in the app, a richer way to show content, and an easy path to what is new, trending and coming soon.

How the leaders handle it

Before designing, I looked at how other streaming apps help people find what is new and what is coming. Public sources and the app listings show a clear pattern: they turn “what’s next” into something you can act on.

Netflix

What it does: a Latest tab, later called New & Popular, with rows for new this week, coming this week and next week, Top 10 and Coming Soon. A Remind Me bell lets you follow a title, and Top 10 titles carry a badge wherever they appear.

What we take: time-based grouping, and a one-tap reminder.

JioHotstar

What it does: its App Store listing describes a Remind Me option for upcoming shows and movies, and live sports are a core draw in India.

What we take: reminders belong to sports as much as to shows.

Sony LIV

What it does: a broad catalogue of originals, reality and live sports, from cricket and football to WWE, with features such as multi-camera viewing. Our internal comparison described the New & Hot layout as a simple carousel with banners: clean and familiar.

The gap: little sense of occasion, anticipation or “this is for you”.

Industry writing on OTT discovery points the same way. Good personalisation should reduce the effort needed to find something worth watching, and works best when editorial curation sits alongside automated recommendations.

Sources: Netflix, Coming Soon feature · DigitalTV Europe, New & Popular tab · Cordcutters, Top 10 badges · JioHotstar, App Store · Sony LIV, App Store · Alpha Networks, OTT discovery

The idea: make “what’s next” a habit

Together, the benchmark and these patterns led to one idea: New & Hot should not be a shelf of banners. It should be a calendar of occasions that tells you what is on now, today, this week and soon, and lets you act in one tap.

01

Group by time, not by type

Live now, new today, this week, coming soon. Users think in moments, so the page follows the same order.

02

Make anticipation actionable

Every upcoming card carries a countdown and Remind Me, so a premiere becomes an appointment.

03

Lead with a hero, then personalise

An editorial hero sets the occasion. Rows below can adapt, for example “since you last visited”.

04

Sports first when it is live

Live matches rise to the top with a live badge, then upcoming fixtures, then highlights.

05

Show proof of what is hot

Trending cues add social proof. The brief marked this for a later phase, pending evaluation.

06

Let the content play

Auto-trailers bring the page to life, with a nudge to opt in and a fallback when they are off.

What the redesign had to do

  • Show a clear cue on the home screen, with a hero for what is live, premiering or top.
  • Offer tabs that stay put and remember where the user was: Trending, New Releases, Upcoming and Sports.
  • Make upcoming content actionable, with a countdown and a one-tap “Remind Me”.
  • Put sports in a natural order: live, upcoming, then highlights.
  • Use scannable cards with a short synopsis, badges and quick actions.
  • Offer a dismissible subscription prompt in context, without losing the user’s place.

The redesigned experience

Designed for every state

  • Skeleton loaders while content loads
  • Placeholders when an image does not load
  • Scroll states for the sticky header and tabs
  • Cards with and without badges, and “Coming soon”
  • Hover cards on web

Solution 03: Search experience redesign

From an empty search experience to an intelligent content discovery destination.

Search expected users to arrive with a title in mind, but users have different intentions. The design question was how to make the search page useful before a single letter is typed.

The previous experience

The earlier search page showed a search field, a few recent searches and a wall of popular posters. It worked for people who knew what to type, and offered less to people who were just looking.

The zero-query state becomes a destination

  • Recent searches come first with a clear all action, so returning users can repeat a search in one tap.
  • Trending searches follow, with filter chips for All, Movies, TV Shows, Originals and Sports, so exploring takes one tap.
  • Poster tiles are large and easy to scan, so users can pick without typing.
  • Results lead with the best match, with a Watch Now action and related videos.
  • The page also handles loading and no-match states.

An empty search box is not a blank page. It is the best chance to show people what they didn’t know they wanted.

One experience, six platforms

Each platform was designed for how people hold, watch and navigate on it. These are not scaled-up phone screens.

Mobile: Android and iOS

Touch, one hand, short sessions. Sticky tabs, large cards and chips sit within thumb reach, and the bottom navigation keeps New & Hot and Search one tap away.

Tablet

Android tablets get more of each tray in view and larger hero cards, with the same tabs and chips as the phone.

iPad

More room means richer layouts: larger heroes and more content per row, with the first fold designed on its own.

TV

A remote, a ten-foot view. Focus states carry the interaction, the keyboard sits beside the results, and the left menu and tabs are navigated with direction keys.

Web

Pointer and hover. Cards respond on hover, the menu stays on the left, and wide screens show more content per row.

Design impact and outcome

The outcomes below describe what the work delivered. Measured results are not included, and the metrics listed at the end could be tracked in a future evaluation.

  • A simpler design systemOne organised foundation of Foundations, Components and Patterns, with consistent documentation and a changelog.
  • Easier to find and reuseComponents with states for every platform, so designers and developers work from the same reference.
  • A more engaging New & HotStronger hierarchy, richer cards, tabs, reminders and trailer nudges.
  • A better search startA zero-query state with recent and trending content, and a top result that leads with the best match.
  • Support for exploringFilter chips and poster tiles for people who don’t yet know what to watch.
  • One experience on six platformsA shared visual language, with layout and interaction adapted to each device.

Metrics that could be measured later: New & Hot visits and tap-through, search initiation rate, search-to-play rate, zero-query interactions, reminder set rate, and design system adoption.

Key learnings

  • Simplicity pays off in a design system. A system people can find their way around gets used, and one that is hard to read gets worked around.
  • Discoverability is a core job in OTT. Great content that nobody sees is the same as no content.
  • Search must serve both behaviours. People who know what they want and people who don’t both need a way in.
  • A zero-query state is an opportunity. It is the best moment to inspire.
  • Consistency needs platform thinking. The same experience means different layouts for touch, a remote and a mouse.

Great experiences don’t just help users find what they’re looking for. They also help them discover what they didn’t know they were looking for.
